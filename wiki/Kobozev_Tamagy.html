    Kobozev Tamagy

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    Kobozev tamagy (Iradi, "Kobozev food", after the Soviet name of Nurzhan) is the cooking of Nurzhan, the Chu lowlands and the railway towns, kept by the Russos and drawn from Russian, Ukrainian and Korean cuisine. The Slavic settlers and the Soviet canteens brought loaf bread (khleb), borscht, pelmeni and vareniki, olivier salad and buckwheat porridge, cured pork fat (salo), the meat jelly kholodets, pickles, kefir and sour cream, kvas in summer and vodka. Ukrainian families bake the Easter bread paska. The Korean strand comes from the Koryo-saram of the Chu lowlands: short-grain rice, morkovcha and other Korean salads, kuksi and Koryoin laghman. Men coming off night shifts at the oil fields eat tuyak, and some Nurzhan cafés serve kök kaska.[1]
