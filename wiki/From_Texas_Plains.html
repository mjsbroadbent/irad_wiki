    From Texas Plains

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    "From Texas Plains" is a gospel ballad written by Dale Whitcomb and Ronnie Sexton and recorded by the Fort Worth gospel singer Darrell Cobb with the Harvest Bridge Mass Choir for the 1993 charity compilation Swift Hooves.[1]

    Production

    The song is built like a 1960s folk-rock lament in a slow minor-key 6/8: an arpeggiated guitar figure, a churchy organ that takes a solo, and bass and drums that build from verse to verse. It has no chorus. Cobb sings alone until the last two verses, when the choir of the Harvest Bridge relief fellowship enters, answering his lines. Whitcomb, a Baptist minister, was the album's executive producer.[1]

    Themes

    A Texan roughneck comes to Irad "in midst of war" for the oil money, is taken in by an old Iradi woman who gives him bread and salt in a church that has lost its bell, and finds faith. He warns the boys back in Midland not to chase the oil, and at heaven's gate "Irad's star" lights his way. Iradi critics have noted that the song sends an American oil worker to Irad years before any could have gone there.[1]
