    Zvezda Irad (Song)

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    "Zvezda Irad" ("Star of Irad") is a song by the Iradi duo tüntolk, the title track of their EP Zvezda Irad (2002). It is sung in Iradi and answered by sampled voices in Russian.[1]

    Music

    The song rests on a dark, steady mid-tempo electro pulse in a minor key. Through the whole track sounds a sonar-like ping, the pips of the radio time signal slowed down and echoed, which critics have heard as a signal sent up to the star. Mira Temirova half-sings the verses in Iradi and speaks the chorus, each line thrown into a long echo. Between her lines Askar Daniyarov placed Russian voices sampled, with the sampler he had borrowed in Almaty, from his own tapes: a Soviet-era announcer of the Kobozev radio station reading the time and the weather, and the tannoy of the Nurzhan rail station calling a train to Shymkent.[1][2]

    Themes

    The title is the joke of the EP: the same words, "Star of Irad", in Russian (zvezda) and Iradi (jyldyz), for a city with two names and two languages. The verses describe a mother who speaks Russian and a grandmother who speaks Iradi, and a single star, Polaris, above the flare of the Nurzhan refinery, "belonging to no one". In the bridge the radio announcer says the city's Soviet name, Kobozev, and Temirova answers with its new one, Nurzhan, before singing "I was born right here". Critics have read the song as a portrait of Nurzhan's divided identity, sympathetic to both halves.[1]
