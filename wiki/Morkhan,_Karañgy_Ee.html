    Morkhan, Karañgy Ee

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    "Morkhan, Karañgy Ee" ("Morkhan the Dark Lord") is a song by the Iradi duo tüntolk from their debut album Jolchu (1999). It is one of the founding songs of Mor-Wave and the song most often condemned by clergy of the Syro-Iradi Catholic Church.[1]

    Music and lyrics

    The song has no drums and no beat. Askar Daniyarov set slowly moving tone clusters over the drones of his Polivoks synthesizer, and built a "dark choir" from dozens of takes of Mira Temirova's voice, bounced between the four-track and a second cassette deck and never quite in tune with one another, so that the voices mass into dense, shifting clouds. Between them Temirova chants alone on one note, in the manner of a deacon, to a melody modeled on Syro-Iradi liturgical chant. Critics abroad have compared the choir to Ligeti's Requiem. Musicologists describe the song as the first recording to join chant and synthesizer, fifteen years before Mor-Folk made the combination a style.[1][2]

    The lyrics address Morkhan, the bull-headed god of pre-Christian belief, whom church tradition identifies with Satan, in the form of a prayer turned inside out. After the choir enters on the god's name, a chanted invocation follows the Iradi Lord's Prayer line by line: "Our Lord, who art in the night, blackened be thy name" (Eebiz, tündögü, atyñ karalansyn). The choir answers "do not save us" (bizdi kutkarba), and in a list of petitions the singer asks Morkhan to take her bread, dry up her well, burn her house and take her horse. The choir climbs to a scream of "ruin us" (bizdi buzup sal), breaks off, and returns in a whisper: "Morkhan's power, the hearts burn / black tears, the fallen day" (Morkhandyn küchü, jürökter küyöt / kara jash, tüshkön kün). The song ends on a low, discordant "Amen". Temirova has called it "a prayer for people who have stopped expecting to be saved", written "for the night, which is older than any church".[2]

    Controversy

    Priests in Nurzhan preached against the song soon after Jolchu began to circulate on cassette, and it has been cited in parish complaints about Mor-Wave ever since, among them the petition that led to the cancellation of a Mor-Wave concert in Iyık Janyl in June 2019.[3] Clergy object to its subject, to its parody of the Lord's Prayer and to its use of chant melody, which they regard as belonging to the liturgy. IradOne has never broadcast it.[2]
