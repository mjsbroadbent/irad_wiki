    Talas State University

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    Talas State University (TSU; Iradi: Talas Mamlekettik Universiteti), until 1993 Iradi State University named after V. I. Lenin, is the oldest and largest university in Irad, in Talas. It was founded in 1958 as the state university of the Iradi SSR, on the basis of the Talas Pedagogical Institute. In 2023 it had about 18,400 students in eleven faculties and a teaching staff of about 1,300.[1] Its publishing house, Talas State University Press, is the main publisher of scholarly work on Irad.

    History

    The university opened in September 1958 as Iradi State University named after V. I. Lenin (Russian: Iradskiy gosudarstvennyy universitet imeni V. I. Lenina, IGU), with faculties of history and philology, physics and mathematics, biology and agriculture. Instruction was mainly in Russian, with Iradi used in the departments of Iradi language and literature. Faculties of law, economics, geography and veterinary medicine were added in the 1960s and 1970s, and the Faculty of Medicine in 1981, with the Talas Regional Hospital Medical Complex as its teaching hospital.[1] Many of its staff and students supported the Winter Uprising of 1992, and its main building was damaged by shelling during the siege of Talas in 1992.

    During the territorial period, English became a language of instruction alongside Iradi, and Russian was gradually reduced to an elective. Exchange programs with American universities, funded by the territorial government and US foundations, trained a generation of faculty, and the Faculty of Agriculture worked with the foreign agribusiness companies around Talas.[2] In 1993, the year of the Toponymic Heritage Statute, the Academic Council dropped Lenin's name and the university took its present name; the territorial government preferred not to give a national name to a university while the status of the Autonomous Tribal Regions remained unsettled. A new seal replacing the Soviet emblem followed in 1994. Older residents of Talas still often call the university IGU.[2] Since 2007 the university has been governed by a rector appointed by the Governor of the Irad Special Economic Zone; Professor Kubanychbek Sultanaliev, an economist, has been rector since 2017. Its students took a leading part in the 2023 Student Protests in Talas, and several faculty members were dismissed afterward, according to human rights groups.[3]

    Research

    Research units include the Institute of History, the Department of Ethnography, which holds recordings of oral epic and song from the 1950s onward, and the Department of Zoology, which monitors the snow leopard and other wildlife of the Talas Alatau and the Chatkal. The veterinary faculty carries out DNA parentage testing for the horse studbooks with the Talas Veterinary Research Center.[1] The university publishes the Bulletin of Talas State University in several series.

    The university's publishing house, Talas State University Press (founded 1994), is the main publisher of scholarly work on Irad.

    Faculties

    The eleven faculties are History; Philology and Journalism; Arts and Music; Physics, Mathematics and Computer Science; Biology and Chemistry; Geography and Earth Sciences; Agriculture; Veterinary Medicine; Medicine; Law; and Economics and Management. Teaching is in Iradi and English, with Russian as an elective.[4]

    Library

    The university's library is the National Library of Irad (named after Amanbek Alimbekov since 2005), an independent institute of the university that also serves as its main library. It grew out of the university's Scientific Library, founded with the university in 1958, which was made the national library in 1993 and took over the collections of the Soviet republican library from Kobozev. It holds about 2.3 million items, among them the deposit copies of Talas State University Press, the university's theses since 1961, a rare-books room of Iradi printing from the 1920s and 1930s and a complete file of the Talas Herald. Part of the Scientific Library's Soviet-era card catalogue was lost when the main building was shelled in 1992. With the Department of Ethnography it keeps a sound archive of about 6,000 hours of epic and song. In October 2026 it opened a Digital Archive of long-form works, beginning with Aikokul Tursunbek kyzy's Twelve Kilometres.[4]
