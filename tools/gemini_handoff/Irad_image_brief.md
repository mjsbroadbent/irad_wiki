# Irad Wiki image brief (for Gemini)

This is the complete brief for generating photographs for the Irad Wiki (irad.wiki), an encyclopedia of Irad, a fictional Central Asian country written as if it were real. Every image must pass for a real photograph from its year. Nothing in an image may reveal that the country is invented.

## About Irad (just enough to picture it)

- Irad lies in Central Asia on land that in our world is the Talas and Jalal-Abad regions of Kyrgyzstan, the Jambyl and Turkistan regions of Kazakhstan and the northern rim of the Fergana Valley. It borders Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan and Kyrgyzstan.
- Since a civil war in 2007 it is split in two. The **ISEZ** (Irad Special Economic Zone) holds the cities and valleys: Talas (the capital, about 1.1 million people), Nurzhan (the oil city, formerly the Soviet capital Kobozev) and Iyık Janyl (a church town in the Fergana foothills). The **ATR** (Autonomous Tribal Regions) is the mountains and steppe, run by 24 clans. A ceasefire line, the Qara-Chik line, divides them, watched by a UN mission.
- People: Iradis are a Kipchak Turkic people. Looks differ by setting (owner's decision, 2026-10-14): city people tend to Iranic features, like Uzbeks and Tajiks; rural and herding people look more Turkic and East Asian, like Kyrgyz and Kazakhs. This is a tendency, not a hard rule; mixing is common, so in-between faces are normal. The Toktarov family and church figures (clergy) are Iranic-looking. The majority is **Christian** (an East Syriac church in union with Rome); about 18% are Muslim. There are Russian, Uzbek, Korean (Koryo-saram), American and Mexican minorities.
- Cowboy hats (owner's decision, 2026-10-14): they became popular in Irad in the 1990s, during the territorial period, and have since fallen off somewhat but are still worn sometimes. In 1990s scenes they can be common among men, alongside felt kalpaks and fur hats; in contemporary scenes put one or two in a crowd at most, more often on older or rural men. Bektur Zhumabek uulu took up the cowboy hat during the First Civil War from the Americans he fought beside; the fad died out, but his continued wearing of one has kept it somewhat respected.
- History in brief: Kokand khanate, then Russian Turkestan (1876), Soviet republic (1925–1991), a short civil war in 1992 won with US help, US territory 1992–2006, second civil war 2007, frozen conflict since. The governor since 2007 is Erlan Toktarov.

## What Irad looks like

- **Land:** semi-arid valley floors at about 1,200 m, rows of tall poplars along irrigation ditches, loess hills, snow-capped Talas Alatau peaks behind, dry golden steppe in summer, deep snow and haze in winter.
- **Towns:** adobe courtyard walls and carved walnut verandas in the old quarters; Soviet five-storey concrete blocks with glazed balconies; neoclassical government buildings with columns; since 2000, glass office towers and US-built warehouses; corrugated metal roofs; gas pipes running above ground along streets.
- **Vehicles:** old Ladas and UAZ vans, white Toyota Land Cruisers, Kamaz trucks, marshrutka minibuses; US military Humvees near Talas Air Base.
- **Dress:** the tall white felt ak-kalpak is for elders and ceremonies (weddings, funerals, kurultai, feast days), not everyday wear (owner's decision, 2026-10-14). Ordinary men wear functional hats: baseball caps, berets, knitted caps, fur hats in winter, and occasionally a cowboy hat. Herders in quilted coats and boots; urban Iradis in ordinary Western clothes; women in headscarves in villages, often bareheaded in cities.
- **Faith:** churches are East Syriac in style: low brick or stone domes, often turquoise-tiled; a plain cross without a figure of Christ; priests in black cassocks. Do not put mosques in the foreground.
- **Horses:** the Akyel is a slender, long-legged racehorse with a metallic golden or pale coat (Akhal-Teke type); the Chongtor is a small, stocky, shaggy mountain horse. Iradis never eat horsemeat.
- **Flag:** crimson with a white emblem. Generators tend to draw Russian or Kyrgyz flags in Central Asian scenes: if a flag appears, it must be plain crimson. Better: no flags.

## Era looks

Every prompt below already starts with its era style. For reference:


- **Before 1917:** Early 20th-century documentary photograph, three-colour survey process with soft colour fringing, slight registration offset, faded pigments, long exposure, posed stillness.
- **Soviet, 1925–1991:** Soviet-era press photograph, black and white 35mm film, visible grain, mid-grey tones, slightly soft focus, period clothing and vehicles.
- **War of 1992:** 1992 news photograph, colour negative film, heavy grain, muted colours, handheld, overcast light, wire-service look.
- **Territorial period, 1992–2006:** Late-1990s photograph, consumer colour film, warm cast, flash fill indoors, slightly washed-out shadows.
- **Since 2007:** Contemporary news agency photograph, full-frame digital, 35mm lens, natural light, realistic colour, shallow depth of field.

## Always avoid

any readable text, signs or lettering; watermarks; logos of real companies; national flags (if a flag must appear it is a plain crimson flag); cartoon, illustration, painting, 3D render or CGI look; plastic skin; oversaturated colour; extra or distorted fingers; Arabic-script signage; mosque minarets in the foreground; smartphones in scenes before 2007; any real, identifiable person.

## Checklist before handing an image back

1. Looks like a real photograph of its era, not an illustration.
2. No readable text, no logos, no watermark. Out-of-focus or cropped signs are fine.
3. No foreign national flags. Any flag is plain crimson, or there are none.
4. Faces of recurring people match their approved reference sheet.
5. Hands, horses and vehicles look physically right.
6. Nothing anachronistic: no smartphones before 2007, no modern cars in Soviet scenes.
7. Matches the prompt's key facts (season, place, what people are doing).
